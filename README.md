# 👥 IBM HR Analytics - Employee Attrition & Workforce Analysis

## 📌 **Project Overview**

This project performs HR data analysis using Python to explore employee attrition, compensation, job roles, employee satisfaction, experience, and workforce characteristics.

The goal is to transform employee data into meaningful insights that can help understand workforce patterns and identify factors associated with employee attrition.

---

## 🎯 **Business Objectives**

The analysis aims to:

- Understand overall employee attrition
- Identify departments and job roles with higher attrition
- Analyze employee compensation and income patterns
- Explore employee satisfaction and work-life balance
- Study the relationship between overtime and attrition
- Analyze employee experience and tenure
- Identify workforce characteristics and patterns
- Generate data-driven HR insights and recommendations

## 💡 **Key Insights**

The analysis identified several important factors associated with employee attrition:

- 📊 The overall employee attrition rate is **16.12%**.
- 👥 The dataset contains **1,470 employees**, of whom **237 left** and **1,233 stayed**.
- ⏰ Employees working overtime have a significantly higher attrition rate (**30.53%**) compared with employees who do not work overtime (**10.44%**).
- 🏢 Attrition varies across departments, with **Sales** showing higher attrition than Research & Development.
- 👔 Attrition differs across job roles, with some roles showing considerably higher employee turnover.
- 😊 Employees who left generally reported lower satisfaction scores than employees who stayed.
- 📈 Employees who left generally had shorter tenure at the company and fewer years in their current roles.
- 💰 Compensation varies substantially across job roles and job levels.
- 🎯 Workload management, employee satisfaction, career development, and early-tenure support are important areas for improving employee retention.

## 📌 **Business Recommendations**

Based on the analysis, the following HR strategies are recommended:

### 1. **Reduce Excessive Overtime**

Monitor employee workload and overtime levels to reduce burnout and improve employee retention.

### 2. **Focus on High-Attrition Departments**

Develop targeted retention strategies for departments with relatively high employee attrition.

### 3. **Address High-Attrition Job Roles**

Identify the reasons behind higher turnover in specific job roles and develop role-specific retention programs.

### 4. **Improve Employee Satisfaction**

Conduct regular employee feedback surveys and address workplace concerns related to job satisfaction and work-life balance.

### 5. **Strengthen Career Development**

Provide employees with:

- Training opportunities
- Mentoring programs
- Career progression paths
- Internal mobility opportunities

### 6. **Support Early-Tenure Employees**

Strengthen onboarding, mentoring, and engagement programs for employees in the early stages of their careers.

### 7. **Review Compensation Structures**

Regularly evaluate compensation across job roles, job levels, and experience levels to maintain fair and competitive pay.

### 8. **Improve Manager-Employee Relationships**

Encourage effective communication, mentoring, and regular feedback between managers and employees.

## 📊 **Analysis Results**

### 📉 **Overall Attrition**

The dataset contains:

- **1,470 total employees**
- **1,233 employees who stayed**
- **237 employees who left**
- **16.12% overall attrition rate**

### ⏰ **Overtime and Attrition**

| Overtime Status | Attrition Rate |
|---|---:|
| Yes | 30.53% |
| No | 10.44% |

Employees working overtime have approximately three times the attrition rate of employees who do not work overtime.

### 😊 **Employee Satisfaction**

Employees who left generally reported lower average satisfaction scores across several workplace satisfaction measures.

### 📈 **Experience & Tenure**

Employees who left generally had shorter organizational tenure and fewer years in their current roles.

These results indicate that organizational tenure, employee satisfaction, workload, and career progression may be relevant factors in employee retention.
